# Amino Acids

20 amino acids build proteins in the body. Split by whether the body can synthesize them:

- **Essential (9)** — can't be synthesized, must come from food: histidine, isoleucine, leucine, lysine, methionine, phenylalanine, threonine, tryptophan, valine.
  - Older references say 8 — histidine was confirmed essential later (adults over the long term, infants especially).
- **Nonessential (11)** — the body synthesizes these, often from essential amino acids or metabolic intermediates.
- **Conditionally essential** — normally nonessential, but demand can outpace synthesis under illness, stress, or rapid growth (e.g. arginine, glutamine).

Common misconception: "essential" does NOT mean "the ones you build everything else from." It means "the ones you can't make yourself, so you must eat them."

## Nonessential synthesis pathways

Most nonessential amino acids branch off three hubs: glycolysis, the TCA cycle, and (for a couple) an essential amino acid as precursor. The general mechanism is **transamination** — moving an amino group onto a carbon skeleton.

**From glycolysis**
- Serine ← 3-phosphoglycerate (oxidized, transaminated, dephosphorylated)
- Glycine ← serine (loses one carbon, folate-dependent)
- Cysteine ← serine + sulfur from methionine (essential) — so cysteine synthesis depends on dietary methionine
- Alanine ← pyruvate (direct transamination)

**From the TCA cycle**
- Aspartate ← oxaloacetate (transamination)
- Asparagine ← aspartate (+ amide group, nitrogen donated by glutamine)
- Glutamate ← alpha-ketoglutarate (transamination)
- Glutamine ← glutamate (+ amide group; key nitrogen shuttle, heavily used by dividing cells)
- Proline ← glutamate (cyclization)

**From an essential amino acid**
- Tyrosine ← phenylalanine (hydroxylation). If phenylalanine intake is inadequate, tyrosine synthesis suffers too — basis of PKU (phenylketonuria), where the converting enzyme is defective.

**Other**
- Arginine — made via the urea cycle (nitrogen clearance pathway), but production often can't keep up during growth/illness → classified conditionally essential.
