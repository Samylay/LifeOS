# What makes an essay good?

## Working definition

A good essay accomplishes a specific purpose for a specific audience. It
answers or productively reframes its central question, advances a clear
controlling idea, and earns its conclusion through relevant evidence and
explicit reasoning. Its structure helps the reader follow that thinking. Its
language is precise and readable, its sources are represented and credited
accurately, and its mechanics do not obstruct meaning.

There is no context-free essay checklist. The prompt, genre, audience, purpose,
source requirements, and instructor or publication rubric define what success
means.

## Review rubric

| Dimension | Weight | What strong work does |
|---|---:|---|
| Task fulfillment and scope | 15 | Performs the requested act, answers the central question, covers explicit constraints, and stays within a manageable scope. |
| Thesis, purpose, and stakes | 12 | Gives the essay a controlling answer or idea that is specific, supportable, and worth the reader's attention. |
| Reasoning, development, and depth | 18 | Connects claims to conclusions, exposes assumptions, explains implications, and develops important ideas beyond assertion or summary. |
| Evidence quality and integration | 15 | Uses relevant and credible evidence, distinguishes fact from inference, represents sources fairly, and explains what each piece of evidence proves. |
| Organization, coherence, and paragraph function | 13 | Gives each section and paragraph a discernible job, orders those jobs logically, sustains focus, and makes relationships clear. |
| Counterarguments and limitations | 7 | Engages the strongest reasonable objection, alternative interpretation, or boundary condition when the genre and claim call for it. |
| Audience, voice, style, and lexical precision | 8 | Supplies the context its reader needs and uses tone, terminology, sentence variation, and word choice appropriate to the purpose. |
| Sentence clarity and mechanics | 7 | Controls syntax, grammar, usage, spelling, and punctuation so errors rarely impede comprehension. |
| Originality and insight | 5 | Contributes a meaningful interpretation, synthesis, distinction, consequence, or solution while separating the writer's ideas from sources. |

Vocabulary quality means precision and fit, not rarity. Length, paragraph
counts, transition-word counts, sentence complexity, and rare vocabulary are
diagnostic signals at most. They should never earn points directly.

## How a review tool should work

The tool should first capture the assignment contract: prompt or question,
essay type, audience, purpose, constraints, source rules, and any custom rubric.
Without that context, it should label its feedback as general guidance.

Every finding should include:

- the relevant passage;
- the rubric criterion;
- the suspected issue;
- why it matters;
- one revision question or action;
- confidence and whether human or source verification is required.

Mechanical checks can reliably flag spelling, punctuation, broken references,
repetition, and some citation problems. Prompt fulfillment, thesis quality,
claim-evidence links, logical gaps, counterarguments, depth, coherence, tone,
and originality require language-model or human judgment.

The tool should distinguish errors from preferences and assignment conventions.
It should diagnose before rewriting, preserve the writer's voice, and avoid an
authoritative overall score. A polished off-prompt essay must not receive a
reassuring result.

## Product path

1. **Finished-draft review:** return a reverse outline, dimension-by-dimension
   assessment, passage-level evidence, confidence, and the three highest-impact
   revision priorities.
2. **Argument workspace:** add a claim-to-evidence-to-analysis map, source and
   citation verification, counterargument testing, and before/after comparison.
3. **Real-time coach:** track prompt requirements, thesis, paragraph jobs,
   claims, evidence, and open questions while the draft changes. Run mechanical
   checks continuously, but wait for a pause or request before interpretive
   feedback so drafting stays interruption-free.

The product should be an argument and revision coach, not an authoritative
grader, rug-pull rewriter, plagiarism accuser, or AI-text detector.

## Risks and safeguards

- Automated scorers can reward length, formula, memorized phrases, or surface
  complexity while missing incoherence. Test with short excellent essays, long
  empty essays, shuffled paragraphs, contradictions, and fabricated citations.
- Models can inherit bias from human ratings. Preserve dialect and multilingual
  voice, validate across relevant groups, and provide human override.
- Plausible feedback can still be wrong. Quote evidence, expose confidence,
  allow “cannot assess,” and keep the tool formative.
- Similarity does not establish plagiarism or intent. Flag possible overlap for
  review without making misconduct claims.

## Open calibration questions

The writer or assignment must decide whether the thesis needs to be explicit,
whether first person, headings, narrative openings, or citations are allowed,
what counts as authoritative evidence, which English variety or style guide
applies, and whether counterargument or a prescribed structure is required.
